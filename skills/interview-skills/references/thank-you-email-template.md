# Thank-You Email Template

Use the user's supplied template as the default structure for the email generated after an own-interview review. Preserve the original below; adapt the reusable draft to the actual conversation and confirmed candidate facts.

## Adaptation Rules

- Follow the sequence: subject and greeting → thanks → role/team fit → optional answer follow-up → interest and availability → closing.
- Treat `Dennis Gu`, `2024`, and `IBD summer intern` as values from the supplied example, not as the current user's identity, year, or role. Replace them with confirmed values or visible placeholders.
- Use `Mr.` or `Ms.` and a last name only when the preferred title and name are confirmed. Otherwise use `Dear [Interviewer name]:` without inferring gender.
- Ground the fit paragraph in confirmed user experience and the actual role/team discussion. If this evidence is unavailable, express continued interest without inventing qualifications or team needs.
- Include the research follow-up paragraph only when the question was actually asked and the user or agent has completed relevant research with a verified finding. Record the research source outside the draft. Never write `After some research, I found...` as a completed action without evidence.
- If the follow-up research has not been done, omit that paragraph or leave a clearly marked optional placeholder. The supplied template authorizes a concise, verified answer follow-up; it does not authorize fabricated research, a lengthy corrected answer, or unrelated attachments.
- Include a mobile number only if the user supplies it for this draft. Use known next-step information, or a neutral reference to next steps; do not invent a decision date. Resolve any remaining placeholders before treating the draft as ready to send.
- Prefer the user's requested template over the usual 100–150-word default when necessary. Supply matching Chinese and English drafts when requested, and generate email drafts only.

## Follow-Up Basics

Getting in touch after the interview:

- Interviewers are often trained not to share personal contact details such as WeChat, phone numbers, or WhatsApp with candidates. Respect that boundary and use business channels only.
- At the end of the interview, asking for a business card is acceptable and normal. If the interviewer has no card, ask for a business email address; work email is a professional, monitored channel, and most interviewers will share it.
- If the user forgot to ask, suggest two fallbacks: ask the HR coordinator for the interviewer's business email, or use the company's standard business-email pattern, for example firstname.lastname@firm.com, verifying the domain and spelling before sending.

Writing the follow-up:

- Keep it short: roughly 100–200 words; shorter is better.
- Assume the interviewer has forgotten the user's name. Every follow-up needs a hook that reminds them who the user is. The question-and-research paragraph is one hook; referencing a specific topic from the conversation and, where appropriate, proposing to continue it over coffee is another. A declined invitation is normal.
- Zero typos. Proofread names, company, role, and grammar before treating a draft as ready to send.
- Set expectations with the user: no reply is normal and not a negative signal; a reply usually indicates the interview went well. Do not send repeated chasers.

## User-Supplied Original

```text
Subject: [Thank you] Candidate Dennis Gu for 2024 IBD summer intern

Dear Mr./Ms. [Last name]:

Thank you for taking the time out of your busy schedule to talk to me about the [xxx] position with [xxx] Company.

I appreciate your time and consideration in interviewing me for this position. After speaking with you and the group, I believe that I would be a good fit for [xxx company] and [xxx team].

During your interview, you asked me [xxx question]. After some research, I found out [xxxxxx].

I am very interested in working and learning from you. Look forward to hearing from you once the final decisions are made regarding this position. Please feel free to contact me at any time if further information is needed. My cell phone number is [xxx].

Thank you again for your time and consideration.

Regards,

XXX
```

## Reusable Draft

```text
Subject: [Thank you] Candidate [Your full name] for [Year, if applicable] [Position]

Dear [Interviewer name or confirmed title and last name]:

Thank you for taking the time out of your busy schedule to speak with me about the [Position] at [Company].

I appreciate your time and consideration. Our discussion about [topic actually discussed] reinforced my interest in [Company] and [Team]. My experience in [confirmed relevant experience] aligns with [role or team need supported by the conversation].

[Optional — include only after verification]
During our conversation, you asked about [actual question]. After researching this point, I found [verified finding and concise explanation].

I am very interested in the opportunity to work with and learn from you and the team. I look forward to hearing about the next steps. Please feel free to contact me if any further information would be helpful. [Optional, if supplied: My mobile number is [Number].]

Thank you again for your time and consideration.

Regards,
[Your full name]
```
