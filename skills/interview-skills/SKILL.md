# Interview Skills

## Overview

Build source-backed interview preparation briefs that help the user understand the company, the role, and the people they may meet. The goal is to help the user speak concretely, ask sharper questions, and avoid generic interview answers.

## When to Use

Use this skill when the user asks for:

- Interview preparation for a specific company or role
- Company background, business model, financial, culture, founder, or leadership research
- Recent company news or strategic developments before an interview
- Role analysis from a job description
- Role-specific market context, such as current trends affecting product, engineering, finance, investing, operations, sales, or strategy roles
- Public, professional interviewer background research
- Questions to ask a recruiter, hiring manager, team member, executive, or interviewer

## Inputs Needed

If the user has not provided enough context, ask for the smallest set of clarifying details needed. Prioritize:

- Company name
- Role title or job description
- Interview stage or format, if known
- Interviewer name or public profile, if known
- User's target outcome, such as recruiter screen, hiring manager round, technical interview, case interview, final round, or executive conversation

If the user asks for a general framework rather than preparation for a specific interview, explain the workflow and offer a reusable checklist.

## Research Standards

Browse the web for current facts unless the user explicitly says not to. Recent company news, leadership, financials, fundraising, product launches, layoffs, litigation, regulation, job postings, and interviewer roles can change quickly.

Prefer primary or high-quality sources:

- Company website, investor relations, annual reports, earnings releases, SEC filings, press releases, and official blog posts
- Job description, career page, company engineering/product/design blogs, and relevant public talks
- Interviewer LinkedIn, company bio, personal website, GitHub, Google Scholar, publications, podcasts, or talks when publicly available
- Reputable business and trade press for recent events and external context

Distinguish confirmed facts from inferences. When information is uncertain or unavailable, say so and explain what would change the prep.

Respect privacy and safety boundaries. Use only public, professional, interview-relevant information for interviewer research. Do not infer protected traits, family details, private contact details, home addresses, or sensitive personal information.

## Workflow

### 1. Clarify the Interview Target

Confirm the company, role, seniority, geography, business unit, and interview stage. If there is a job description, extract the most important responsibilities, required skills, preferred signals, and likely evaluation criteria.

### 2. Research the Company

Build a concise company profile:

- What the company does, core products, customer segments, and business model
- Recent developments, including product launches, leadership changes, funding, partnerships, layoffs, M&A, regulatory issues, and major press
- Financial or operating trends where available: revenue, growth, margins, cash flow, guidance, retention, unit economics, or public-company KPIs
- Competitive position, market dynamics, and the main strategic question the company appears to be facing
- Culture, values, operating principles, and founder or leadership background

### 3. Research the Role

Connect the job to the company's current situation:

- Why this role likely exists now
- What business, product, technical, operational, or market problems the role may be hired to solve
- Which recent role-specific trends matter, such as AI tooling, regulatory shifts, hiring patterns, industry standards, or competitor moves
- What evidence from the user's background should be emphasized for this role

### 4. Research the Interviewer

If the interviewer is known, prepare a professional background check:

- Current role and likely relationship to the open position
- Career path, domain expertise, public work, talks, publications, patents, repos, interviews, or posts
- What they may care about in the interview
- Smart questions tailored to their background

Keep this section professional and restrained. The goal is to understand context, not to create a personal dossier.

### 5. Build Candidate Positioning

Translate research into interview strategy:

- Best-fit experiences from the user's background
- Likely objections or gaps to prepare for
- Two or three concise stories the user should have ready
- A company-specific answer to "Why this company?"
- A role-specific answer to "Why this role?"

### 6. Produce the Prep Brief

Use the structure in [research-checklist.md](references/research-checklist.md). Adapt depth to the user's timeline: quick screen, standard prep, or deep-dive prep.

## Output Format

Unless the user requests another format, produce:

1. Interview snapshot
2. Executive summary
3. Company briefing
4. Recent developments and why they matter
5. Role analysis
6. Interviewer background, if known
7. Candidate positioning
8. Questions to ask
9. Risks, gaps, and prep checklist
10. Sources and evidence quality

## Red Flags

Stop and ask or revise if any of these happen:

- The company or role is ambiguous and multiple interpretations would change the research
- The brief gives generic advice that could apply to any company
- Recent claims are not sourced or dated
- Financial claims are made without filings, investor materials, or clear source labels
- Interviewer research includes private, sensitive, or non-professional details
- Questions to ask are easily answered by the company's homepage
- The role analysis does not connect to the company's current business context

## Verification

Before finishing, check:

- [ ] Company, role, and interview stage are identified or explicitly marked unknown
- [ ] Recent developments are current, sourced, and dated
- [ ] Facts, reported claims, anecdotes, and inferences are labeled clearly
- [ ] Financial or operating analysis is included when relevant and available
- [ ] Role analysis explains why this hire may matter now
- [ ] Interviewer research uses only public professional information
- [ ] The user gets concrete talking points and tailored questions
- [ ] Uncertainty and missing information are called out
